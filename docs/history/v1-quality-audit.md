# Course Video and Notes Quality Audit

This audit is based on direct review of the surviving linear-algebra video, four
fresh MIT 18.703 production renders, the generated Modern Algebra notes, and the
42-page reference document `1231s Definitions and Formulas`.

## Reference Standard

The video target is visual mathematical explanation in the style exemplified by
3Blue1Brown's sphere-area short: one concrete question, persistent mathematical
objects, transformations that reveal the argument, sparse labels, and a formula
that arrives as the summary of the picture. Motion must explain a relation or an
invariant; decorative pulsing and generic diagrams do not count.

The notes target is the supplied discrete-mathematics document: a restrained
LaTeX layout, course-order sections, numbered definitions/theorems/propositions,
fully worked proofs and examples, notation/formula tables, algorithms where
appropriate, and relevant diagrams. Length is evidence of coverage only when the
content is source-grounded and non-repetitive.

## Cross-Domain Acceptance Criteria

Each validation course must use the same workflow and all installed course skills.
The final gate requires:

1. Source pack covers the official syllabus, lectures/notes, assignments, and exams where available.
2. No planned or supplemental topic is unsupported by course evidence or a required prerequisite.
3. Notes compile with a real TeX engine and contain no fabricated terms, placeholder prose, unmatched exercises, broken math, clipped tables, or irrelevant figures.
4. Every video has a concept-specific storyboard and semantic visual family; generic fallback output is not publishable.
5. Final MP4s are silent by explicit intent, 1080x1920, progressive H.264/yuv420p at 30 fps or better.
6. Dense temporal review finds no overlap, clipping, run-together text, unexplained long holds, or decorative-only motion.
7. Worked values, plotted points, transformations, algorithms, and conclusions agree with the source and with one another.
8. A human frame-by-frame review confirms that the animation itself carries the explanation.

The course rotation for regression testing is Modern Algebra, Algorithms,
Classical Mechanics, and Principles of Microeconomics. Additional fixtures cover
linear algebra, probability, chemistry, and game theory so fixes cannot depend on
one notation system or one kind of diagram.
